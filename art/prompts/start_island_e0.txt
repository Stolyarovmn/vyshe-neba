START_ISLAND / E0 environment asset
Create ONE isolated large rocky island terrain asset for the game «Выше неба», and nothing else.

The island must provide enough buildable flat land for all future base-expansion placeables, but NONE of those purchased buildings are present yet.

The only man-made object allowed is the launch table / launch pad with its minimal concrete foundation.

The rest of the island is undeveloped natural terrain: rock shoreline, low vegetation, sparse conifer groups, compact dirt/service paths and broad intentionally empty construction clearings. The island should feel intentionally planned for later development, with several naturally connected flat terraces and a service route between them.

No houses, no hangars, no fuel tanks, no radar, no antennas, no warehouses, no cranes, no port structures, no vehicles, no rocket, no people.

ONE island only. No ocean background, no sky, no distant mountains, no clouds. Transparent background outside the physical island silhouette. The shoreline itself may include a thin band of surf/foam as part of the island silhouette, but no rectangular water backdrop.

No text, labels, numbers, diagrams, UI, infographic, legend, arrows, panels, split-screen, concept sheet or multiple views.

Wide horizontal footprint suitable for side-view gameplay. The island should occupy roughly 1.7–2 gameplay screens horizontally and contain enough separated buildable terraces for the complete base-development layout.

VYSHE NEBA — CANONICAL STYLE BIBLE
Style family: cohesive HD pixel-art / pixel-look game art with deliberate pixel clusters, clear silhouettes, restrained detail and a finished indie-game presentation. It must look designed, not like a filtered 3D render or generic AI illustration.

Shape language: large readable masses first, functional construction second, sparse small accents last. Silhouettes must remain legible at actual gameplay size and when reduced by the camera. Avoid meaningless micro-detail, decorative noise and accidental extra parts.

Shading: stepped pixel shading is the default. Use a small, intentional tonal ramp per material; add extra tones, controlled dithering or a limited gradient only when they clearly improve material readability. No pillow shading and no soft airbrushed volume on solid small sprites.

Palette temperature: shadows are cool dark blue-gray; lit neutrals are warm off-white/metal; saturated reds, golds and cyans are accents, not mandatory identifiers. Materials may use their own palettes as long as value hierarchy and world coherence are preserved.

Line: prefer dark chromatic selective outlines instead of pure black. The outer contour is usually visually about one logical pixel at runtime, may lighten or disappear on the lit side, and may deepen on the shadow side. Interior lines are sparse and functional.

Texture: intentional pixel clusters, stepped diagonals and broad quiet regions. Avoid random single-pixel noise. Dithering is a deliberate material or transition tool, never a blanket texture.

Detail hierarchy: keep most of the surface calm. Concentrate detail around functional mechanisms, focal points, material changes and attachment areas. Readability at gameplay size has priority over master-image detail.

One consistent key light comes from the upper-left, with warm light on upper-left planes and cool blue-gray shadow on lower-right planes; use no baked cast shadow.

Projection: preserve the established side / slight 3/4 environmental projection used by the game. Do not switch to top-down map, isometric management view or frontal concept-art view.

Alpha and composition: clean transparent background, complete uncropped island silhouette and enough transparent padding around the shoreline.

System separation: image contains only the physical island terrain, shoreline/surf and launch table/foundation. Code owns ocean, weather, waves, rain, lighting response, reflections, haze, particles, shadows and all other temporary effects.

Exclusions: no text, letters, numbers, logos, watermark, pseudo-writing, UI, baked glow, bloom, cast shadow, rain, particles, smoke, motion blur, speed lines or frozen effects.
