START_ISLAND_E0 / environment
Create ONE large finite starter-island environment asset, and nothing else.
Low side / slight 3/4 side gameplay camera matching the rocket flight scene, not top-down and not map/isometric.
The island must be broad enough for all future base placeables B01-B07, with several naturally connected flat buildable terraces and access paths.
Only the starter launch table / launch pad is allowed as built infrastructure. No rocket. No hangars, tanks, radar, antennas, warehouses, cranes, docks, vehicles, people, text, UI, diagrams or multiple views.
The physical island contains rocky shoreline, cliffs, sand coves, sparse conifers/low vegetation, dirt/service paths and intentionally empty construction clearings.
No ocean background, no sky, no distant mountains, no clouds. Transparent background outside the physical island silhouette.
Complete uncropped silhouette with padding. One island only.
One consistent key light comes from the upper-left, with warm light on upper-left planes and cool blue-gray shadow on lower-right planes; use no baked cast shadow.

VYSHE NEBA — CANONICAL STYLE BIBLE
Style family: cohesive HD pixel-art / pixel-look game art with deliberate pixel clusters, clear silhouettes, restrained detail and a finished indie-game presentation. It must look designed, not like a filtered 3D render or generic AI illustration.
Shape language: large readable masses first, functional construction second, sparse small accents last. Silhouettes must remain legible at actual gameplay size and when reduced by the camera.
Shading: stepped pixel shading is the default. Use a small, intentional tonal ramp per material. No pillow shading and no soft airbrushed volume on solid small sprites.
Palette temperature: shadows are cool dark blue-gray; lit neutrals are warm off-white/metal; saturated reds, golds and cyans are accents.
Line: prefer dark chromatic selective outlines instead of pure black.
Texture: intentional pixel clusters, stepped diagonals and broad quiet regions. Avoid random single-pixel noise.
Detail hierarchy: keep most of the surface calm. Concentrate detail around functional mechanisms, focal points, material changes and attachment areas.
